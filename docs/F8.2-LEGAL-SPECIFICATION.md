### 1.3 Important Legal Boundary: Art. 116 TRLGDCU

Art. 116 TRLGDCU establishes:

> "El ejercicio de las acciones que contempla este título será **incompatible** con el ejercicio de las acciones derivadas del saneamiento previstas en el Código Civil."

**What this means**: For consumer goods, the TRLGDCU conformity regime (Arts. 114-125) **completely replaces** the Civil Code hidden-defects regime (Arts. 1484-1497). The consumer must choose one or the other — they cannot cumulate both.

**What this means for Resolveo**: The system must never apply CC Arts. 1484-1497 to goods that fall within the TRLGDCU scope. However, Art. 116 also states: "En todo caso, el consumidor o usuario tendrá derecho, de acuerdo con la legislación civil y mercantil, a ser indemnificado por los daños y perjuicios derivados de la falta de conformidad." — meaning the consumer's right to **damages** (indemnización) under general civil/commercial law is preserved. Resolveo does not compute damages, so this reservation is informational only.

## 2. Legal Regimes

### 2.1 Legal Warranty (Garantía Legal) — Arts. 114-125 TRLGDCU

**What it is**: Mandatory, non-waivable right of the consumer against the **seller** (empresario/vendedor) for lack of conformity of goods with the contract. Transposes EU Directive 2019/771.

**Scope** (Art. 114): All sale-of-goods contracts for existing goods or goods to be produced.

**Exclusions** (Art. 114.2): Live animals; second-hand goods at public auctions the consumer attends personally; services (except digital content/services); electronic communications services; gambling; financial services; healthcare digital content; open-source software without payment; cinematic digital projections; reuses of public-sector information.

**Conformity requirements**:

- _Subjective_ (Art. 115 bis): (a) Match description, type, quantity, quality, functionality, compatibility, interoperability per the contract. (b) Fit for specific purposes the consumer communicated and the seller accepted. (c) Delivered with accessories, instructions, and installation/integration assistance per the contract. (d) Delivered with updates per the contract.

- _Objective_ (Art. 115 ter.1): (a) Fit for normal purposes of similar goods, complying with applicable norms/standards/codes of conduct. (b) Match sample or model. (c) Delivered with expected accessories, packaging, and instructions. (d) Possess expected quantity, qualities, and characteristics — including durability, functionality, compatibility, and security — considering any public statements by the seller, manufacturer, or earlier in the supply chain (especially advertising/labelling). The seller may escape liability for public statements only if they can prove: (1) they didn't know and couldn't reasonably have known about the statement; (2) the statement was corrected before the contract; or (3) the statement couldn't have influenced the purchase decision.

**Corrected terminology**: The legal concept is "falta de conformidad con el contrato" (lack of conformity with the contract), NOT "defecto" (defect) in the consumer-safety sense. A product can be non-conforming without being dangerous, and dangerous without being non-conforming in the contractual sense. Resolveo must use the correct terminology throughout.

**Critical distinctions the system must make**:

1. **Responsibility period** (Art. 120.1): The seller IS liable for any lack of conformity that (a) existed at delivery AND (b) manifests within 3 years. If a non-conformity manifests at year 2.5, the consumer CAN claim — even though the 2-year presumption has expired (the consumer bears the burden of proof from year 3 onward).

2. **Presumption period** (Art. 121.1): During the first 2 years from delivery, the non-conformity is presumed to have existed at delivery, UNLESS such presumption is incompatible with the nature of the good or the nature of the non-conformity. After 2 years, the consumer must prove the non-conformity existed at delivery. This is the most important temporal fact for the warranty-rejection module.

3. **Post-repair/replacement** (Art. 122.3): After a conforming good is returned, the seller remains liable for defects of the same origin as the original non-conformity for 1 year from the new delivery. The presumption applies during that year. This creates a new "clock" that extends the consumer's effective protection period.

4. **Nature incompatibility exception** (Art. 121.1 final clause): The 2-year presumption does not apply when it is "incompatible with the nature of the good or the nature of the lack of conformity." This is an open-textured exception (e.g., gradual battery degradation in electronics may be considered inherent). The system cannot determine when this exception applies — it must be flagged as requiring human evaluation.

### 2.3 Presumption of Pre-existing Non-Conformity (Art. 121.1)

**What it establishes**: "Salvo prueba en contrario, se presumirá que las faltas de conformidad que se manifiesten en los dos años siguientes a la entrega del bien [...] ya existían cuando el bien se entregó."

**Duration**: 2 years from delivery for goods.

**Rebuttal**: The seller can rebut the presumption by proving the non-conformity:

- Resulted from misuse, damage by the consumer, or external causes
- Is inherent to the nature of the good or the type of non-conformity (e.g., normal wear, gradual battery degradation)
- Is contradicted by the consumer's own statements (e.g., "I dropped it")

**What the system CAN determine**: Whether the 2-year period has elapsed (factual, date-based).

**What the system CANNOT determine**: Whether the presumption is "incompatible with the nature of the good or the nature of the non-conformity." This requires qualitative legal evaluation.

### 2.4 Putting into Conformity: Repair and Replacement (Art. 118)

**Hierarchy**: Consumer chooses between repair and replacement (Art. 118.1), subject to the seller's right to refuse ONE of them under specific conditions.

**Repair** (Art. 118.4-6):

- Free of charge (includes shipping, labor, materials)
- Must be completed within a reasonable time from when the seller was informed
- Must be carried out without major inconvenience to the consumer
- If the good was installed, repair includes removal and reinstallation at seller's cost
- Consumer provides the goods to the seller; seller recovers replaced goods at their expense

**Replacement** (Art. 118.1, 118.5-7):

- Same conditions as repair (free, reasonable time, no inconvenience)
- Seller may offer a refurbished good only at explicit consumer request
- Seller recovers the non-conforming good at their expense
- Consumer is not liable for normal use of replaced goods during the replacement period

**Seller's right to refuse** (Art. 118.3): The seller may refuse to put the goods into conformity when it is **impossible OR involves disproportionate costs**, considering:

- (a) The value the goods would have had if there had been no lack of conformity
- (b) The relevance of the lack of conformity
- (c) Whether the alternative remedy could be provided without major inconvenience to the consumer

**CRITICAL CORRECTION**: The conditions are **disjunctive** (impossible OR disproportionate), NOT conjunctive (impossible AND disproportionate). The seller needs only ONE ground to refuse.

### 2.5 Price Reduction and Contract Resolution (Art. 119)

The consumer may demand a proportionate price reduction or contract resolution in ANY of the following situations:

**(a)** Repair/replacement is impossible or disproportionate (Art. 118.3)
**(b)** The seller did not carry out repair/replacement, or did not do so in accordance with Arts. 118.5-6 (proper handling, removal/reinstallation), or did not do so within a reasonable time — AND the consumer has requested reduction/resolution
**(c)** — (digital content/services — not applicable to V1)
**(d)** Any lack of conformity appears after the seller's attempt to put the good into conformity
**(e)** The lack of conformity is of such gravity that it justifies immediate price reduction or resolution
**(f)** The seller has declared, or circumstances clearly indicate, that the seller will not put the goods into conformity within a reasonable time or without major inconvenience to the consumer

**CRITICAL LIMITATION**: "La resolución no procederá cuando la falta de conformidad sea de escasa importancia." (Art. 119, final clause, explicitly stated in the TRLGDCU text as transposed.) Resolution does NOT proceed when the non-conformity is of **minor importance**. This is NOT implied — it is an explicit statutory limitation.

**The system must NOT**: Automatically equate "warranty rejected" → "right to resolution." The consumer's right to resolution depends on the specific Art. 119(a)-(f) conditions being met.

### 2.6 "Warranty Rejection" — Legal Decomposition

"El vendedor ha rechazado la garantía" is NOT a legal concept in the TRLGDCU. It is a colloquial description. The system must decompose it into factual components:

1. **Seller denies the existence of non-conformity**: "The product is not defective." → Factual dispute. System CANNOT determine who is right.

2. **Seller admits the defect but denies responsibility**: "The defect exists but is not covered." → May be legitimate (e.g., defect caused by consumer misuse) or illegitimate. System cannot determine without evidence.

3. **Seller refuses repair/replacement citing impossibility or disproportionate costs**: Seller has a legal right to refuse one remedy (Art. 118.3), BUT must then offer the alternative. If both are refused, Art. 119 conditions may apply.

4. **Seller offers only repair when the consumer chose replacement** (or vice versa): Consumer has the legal right to choose (Art. 118.1). Seller's restriction of options may not comply with the law.

5. **Seller rejects without any explanation**: The consumer's rights remain intact. The seller's silence does not extinguish them. Art. 119(f) may apply.

6. **Seller rejects based on a technical report**: The report is evidence, not proof. The consumer can challenge it. Within the 2-year presumption, the burden is on the seller.

7. **Seller rejects after the 2-year presumption period**: The consumer must prove the non-conformity existed at delivery. System cannot resolve this without evidence.

8. **Seller rejects citing expiration of the 3-year responsibility period**: If true, the legal warranty rights may have expired. System must verify dates.

9. **Seller rejects citing commercial warranty expiration**: Commercial warranty is SEPARATE from legal warranty. Its expiration does NOT affect legal warranty rights.

### 2.7 Legal Warranty vs. Commercial Warranty

| Aspect                | Legal Warranty                                                  | Commercial Warranty                                             |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Source**            | TRLGDCU Arts. 114-125                                           | Voluntary commitment by seller/manufacturer                     |
| **Legal basis**       | Mandatory, non-waivable (Art. 10 TRLGDCU)                       | Contractual obligation (guarantee document)                     |
| **Against whom**      | Seller (empresario/vendedor) — the one who sold to the consumer | Guarantor (garante) as identified in the warranty document      |
| **Period**            | 3 years (goods), 2 years (digital)                              | As stated in the warranty document                              |
| **Coverage**          | Any lack of conformity with the contract                        | As stated in the warranty document (may be broader or narrower) |
| **Cannot be limited** | Yes — any clause limiting it is null                            | No — but cannot limit the legal warranty                        |
| **Relationship**      | Independent, primary                                            | Additional, does NOT replace or limit legal warranty            |

**Key distinction**: The commercial warranty is offered by the **guarantor** (which may be the manufacturer, a distributor, or the seller). The legal warranty is always against the **seller** (the one who sold to the consumer). These are different legal persons in most cases.

**System rule**: The existence or non-existence of a commercial warranty does NOT affect the consumer's legal warranty rights. They are tracked independently.

### 2.8 Seller vs. Manufacturer (Art. 125)

**General rule**: The consumer claims against the **seller** (empresario who sold the goods).

**Exception** (Art. 125.1): When it is impossible or disproportionately burdensome for the consumer to contact the seller, the consumer may claim directly against the **manufacturer** (productor) to have the goods put into conformity.

**Manufacturer liability scope** (Art. 125.1): The manufacturer responds for non-conformity relating to "el origen, identidad o idoneidad de los bienes, de acuerdo con su naturaleza y finalidad y con las normas que los regulan."

**Subrogation** (Art. 125.2): The party who responded to the consumer has 1 year to claim against the responsible party (repetition/subrogation).

**V1 handling**: The system tracks whether the claim is against the seller or the manufacturer, but does not manage the subrogation process. The default assumption is seller. Manufacturer claims are flagged as a separate pathway.

### 2.9 Goods with Digital Elements

TRLGDCU Arts. 115 ter.2, 120.2, 121.2-5 regulate goods with digital elements.

**V1 scope decision**: EXCLUDE digital-conformity obligations.

**What is excluded**:

- Obligations regarding digital updates (Art. 115 ter.2)
- Digital-specific burden-of-proof rules (Art. 121.2-5)
- Continuous digital supply periods (Art. 120.2)
- Digital-environment compatibility testing (Art. 121.3-5)

**What remains included**: Physical goods with embedded firmware/software where the conformity issue relates to the PHYSICAL product (e.g., a washing machine whose drum breaks). The system does not evaluate whether firmware updates are required or whether the digital component conforms.

**Rationale**: The digital conformity regime introduces fact requirements (update obligations, environment compatibility, continuous-supply periods) that significantly complicate V1 without addressing the most common consumer warranty-rejection scenarios.

### 2.10 Used Goods (Second-Hand)

**Legal regime**: TRLGDCU applies to second-hand goods (Art. 114 does NOT exclude them, except from public auctions — Art. 114.2.b).

**Key differences** (Arts. 120.1, 121.1):

- The seller and consumer may agree on a shorter responsibility period, but it CANNOT be less than 1 year from delivery.
- If no agreement on a shorter period exists, the standard 3-year period applies.
- The seller and consumer may agree on a shorter presumption period, but it cannot be less than the agreed responsibility period.

**CORRECTION from previous version**: The default for used goods is **3 years** (same as new goods), NOT 1 year. The 1-year minimum only applies when the parties have specifically agreed to reduce the period. The system must NOT default to 1 year when no agreement is documented.

**Facts needed for used goods**:

- `product.condition_at_sale` (new / used / refurbished) — REQUIRED to determine which time regime applies
- `compliance.agreed_reduced_period_months` (optional — only when parties agreed to reduce)

## 3. Fact Model

### 3.1 Fact Categories

Facts are organized into four categories reflecting how they enter the system:

**REQUIRED FACTS** — minimum viable set for any analysis to proceed. Without these, no rule can evaluate.

**OPTIONAL FACTS** — enrich the analysis when available. Rules may produce INSUFFICIENT_DATA or POTENTIALLY_APPLICABLE when these are absent.

**DERIVED FACTS** — computed by the analysis service from other facts + legal rules. Never collected from the user directly.

**EVIDENCE-ONLY FACTS** — facts that may exist in documents but should only enter the system via AI extraction or user confirmation, never assumed.

### 3.11 AI Architecture Constraints

The AI extraction pipeline (F6) operates under strict constraints:

**AI CAN**:

- Interpret natural language descriptions of the defect
- Extract fact candidates from documents (dates, amounts, text)
- Classify documents by type (invoice, rejection letter, warranty card)
- Detect potential contradictions between user statements and documents
- Suggest follow-up questions based on what information is missing
- Explain results in plain language
- Draft correspondence (reclamación letters)

**AI CANNOT**:

- Determine whether a product has a "lack of conformity" — this is a legal assessment that belongs to the Rule Engine
- Confirm that an extracted fact is true — candidates remain UNCONFIRMED until user/system confirmation
- Invent legal articles, sources, or facts
- Determine whether a seller's rejection reason is legally valid
- Determine whether a defect is of "minor importance" (Art. 119 limitation)
- Publish rules or modify the legal state of a case
- Determine whether the nature-incompatibility exception (Art. 121.1) applies

**Critical separation**: `DocumentFactCandidate` ≠ `Fact`. AI extraction produces candidates; only the existing user/system confirmation flow creates Facts (per F2 architecture).

## 4. Remedial Sequence Model

The TRLGDCU establishes a clear remedial sequence. The system must model this correctly:

```
1. Non-conformity exists (falta de conformidad)
   ↓
2. Consumer communicates to seller (notified_seller)
   ↓
3. Consumer requests conformity (repair OR replacement — consumer's choice)
   ↓
4a. Seller performs repair/replacement → conforming good returned
   → If same defect recurs within 1 year → Art. 119.d applies
   → If different defect appears → new non-conformity analysis
   ↓
4b. Seller refuses repair/replacement
   → Grounds: impossible (Art. 118.3)
   → Grounds: disproportionate costs (Art. 118.3)
   → Grounds: declared won't repair (Art. 119.f)
   → Grounds: no response within reasonable time (Art. 119.b)
   ↓
5. Consumer may demand price reduction OR resolution (Art. 119)
   → Only if non-conformity is NOT of minor importance
   → Only if one of Art. 119(a)-(f) conditions is met
```

The system must model the position within this sequence, not jump to conclusions.

## 5. Proposed Rules

### Rule 1: `warranty-rejection.seller-rejected-within-period`

**Purpose**: Determine whether the seller rejected the warranty claim while the product was within the 3-year legal responsibility period.

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `purchase.delivery_date`

**Derived facts needed**: `compliance.current_date`, `compliance.responsibility_deadline`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  DATE_BEFORE(key: compliance.responsibility_deadline)
]
```

Semantics: The responsibility deadline is before today → FALSE means the deadline has NOT passed → we are WITHIN the period.

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "The seller rejected the warranty claim, and the product is within the 3-year legal responsibility period (Art. 120.1 TRLGDCU)."

**Result when POTENTIALLY_APPLICABLE**: Same assertion but with UNCONFIRMED facts involved (e.g., delivery date from AI extraction, not yet user-confirmed).

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "The 3-year legal responsibility period has expired. The seller's rejection, if it occurred after expiry, falls outside the statutory responsibility window."

**Result when INSUFFICIENT_DATA**: "We cannot determine whether the product is within the responsibility period without knowing the delivery date."

**Result when CONTRADICTED**: "The delivery date information is contradictory (e.g., invoice date vs. user statement). Please verify the correct delivery date."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether a non-conformity exists, whether the rejection is legally justified, what remedy the consumer is entitled to.

**Source**: Art. 120.1 TRLGDCU.

**Limitations**: Does not account for Art. 122 (suspension during repair). Does not account for used-goods reduced periods (uses default 3 years unless the agreed period is known via derived fact).

---

### Rule 2: `warranty-rejection.presumption-applies`

**Purpose**: Determine whether the 2-year rebuttable presumption of pre-existing non-conformity applies (Art. 121.1).

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `purchase.delivery_date`

**Derived facts needed**: `compliance.current_date`, `compliance.presumption_deadline`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  DATE_BEFORE(key: compliance.presumption_deadline)
]
```

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "The product is within the first 2 years from delivery. There is a legal presumption that the non-conformity existed at the time of delivery (Art. 121.1 TRLGDCU). The seller bears the burden of proving otherwise."

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "More than 2 years have passed since delivery. The consumer must prove that the non-conformity existed at the time of delivery."

**Result when INSUFFICIENT_DATA**: "We cannot determine whether the 2-year presumption applies without knowing the delivery date."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether the seller has successfully rebutted the presumption. Whether the presumption is "incompatible with the nature of the good" (Art. 121.1 exception — requires human evaluation).

**Source**: Art. 121.1 TRLGDCU.

**Limitations**: The nature-incompatibility exception cannot be evaluated by the system. When this exception is potentially relevant (e.g., battery degradation), the system should flag it as a caveat.

---

### Rule 3: `warranty-rejection.no-remedy-offered`

**Purpose**: Determine whether the seller rejected the warranty claim without offering ANY statutory remedy (repair or replacement).

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `seller.offered_repair`, `seller.offered_replacement`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  BOOLEAN_IS_FALSE(key: seller.offered_repair),
  BOOLEAN_IS_FALSE(key: seller.offered_replacement)
]
```

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "The seller rejected the warranty claim without offering repair or replacement. Under Art. 117 and 118 TRLGDCU, when a lack of conformity exists, the seller must remedy it through repair or replacement (consumer's choice)."

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "The seller has offered at least one remedy (repair or replacement)."

**Result when INSUFFICIENT_DATA**: "We do not have enough information about whether the seller offered any remedy."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether the offered remedy (if any) is adequate, whether the seller's refusal of one remedy while offering the other is lawful, whether the consumer's preferred remedy was available.

**Source**: Arts. 117.1, 118.1 TRLGDCU.

**Limitations**: This rule only fires when NEITHER remedy is offered. It does not cover the scenario where the seller offers one remedy but refuses the consumer's chosen one — that is a separate factual situation requiring human evaluation.

---

### Rule 4: `warranty-rejection.repair-failed-or-defect-recurred`

**Purpose**: Determine whether repair was attempted and either failed or the same defect recurred, which may entitle the consumer to price reduction or resolution under Art. 119.

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `repair.completed`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  ANY [
    BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: repair.failed),
    BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: repair.defect_recurred)
  ]
]
```

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "Repair was attempted but [failed / the same defect recurred]. Under Art. 119 TRLGDCU, the consumer may request a proportionate price reduction or contract resolution, provided the non-conformity is not of minor importance."

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "No evidence of a failed repair or recurred defect."

**Result when INSUFFICIENT_DATA**: "We do not have enough information about the repair history."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether resolution is the appropriate remedy (minor importance exception applies — Art. 119). Whether the consumer has already requested reduction/resolution (Art. 119.b requires the consumer to have requested it).

**Source**: Art. 119.d TRLGDCU (recurrence after conformity attempt); Art. 119.b (failure to repair within reasonable time).

**Limitations**: The distinction between "defect recurred" (same origin) and "different defect appeared" is important — Art. 122.3 only provides the 1-year presumption for same-origin defects. The system should distinguish these when facts are available.

---

### Rule 5: `warranty-rejection.seller-claims-expired`

**Purpose**: Determine whether the seller has specifically claimed the warranty has expired, which is a verifiable factual assertion.

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `seller.claimed_warranty_expired`, `purchase.delivery_date`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.claimed_warranty_expired),
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: purchase.delivery_date)  // We need the date to verify
]
```

Actually, the condition should be simpler — we want to detect when the seller's claim can be verified against the dates:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.claimed_warranty_expired)
]
```

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "The seller has claimed the warranty has expired. We can verify this claim against the delivery date: [verification result from other rules]."

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "The seller has not claimed the warranty has expired."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether the seller's claim is correct — that depends on the actual dates (Rules 1 and 2). This rule simply identifies the seller's stated position.

**Source**: Art. 120.1 TRLGDCU (the period the seller is referencing).

---

### Rule 6: `warranty-rejection.seller-declares-wont-repair`

**Purpose**: Detect when the seller has declared they will not put the goods into conformity, which triggers Art. 119(f).

**Facts required**: `seller.rejection`, `seller.declared_wont_repair`

**Condition**:

```
ALL [
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.rejection),
  BOOLEAN_IS_TRUE(key: seller.declared_wont_repair)
]
```

**Result when SUPPORTED**: "The seller has declared they will not put the goods into conformity. Under Art. 119.f TRLGDCU, this may entitle the consumer to demand price reduction or contract resolution."

**Result when NOT_APPLICABLE**: "The seller has not explicitly declared they will not repair."

**What this rule does NOT conclude**: Whether resolution is appropriate (minor importance exception). Whether the consumer has requested reduction/resolution (Art. 119.b).

**Source**: Art. 119.f TRLGDCU.

## 10. Action Mapping

### 10.1 Actions

| Action Type           | When Triggered                                                              | Priority | Description                                                                                                                |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `COLLECT_INFORMATION` | Required facts missing (delivery date, seller response, etc.)               | 1        | "Provide [specific missing fact] to continue the analysis."                                                                |
| `PRESERVE_EVIDENCE`   | When seller rejection exists                                                | 1        | "Preserve all documentation: invoices, rejection letters, photos, correspondence with the seller."                         |
| `CONTACT_MERCHANT`    | When consumer has not formally notified seller, or seller has not responded | 2        | "Contact the seller in writing to formally notify the defect and request a remedy (repair or replacement)."                |
| `GENERATE_DOCUMENT`   | When key facts are confirmed and a formal claim is needed                   | 3        | "Generate a formal warranty claim letter (reclamación por garantía)."                                                      |
| `SUBMIT_COMPLAINT`    | When seller has rejected or not responded after formal notification         | 4        | "File a formal complaint with the consumer arbitration board (Junta Arbitral de Consumo) or the local consumer authority." |
| `WAIT_FOR_RESPONSE`   | When consumer has contacted seller and is awaiting response                 | 5        | "Wait for the seller's response. If no response within a reasonable time, further action may be appropriate."              |

### 10.2 Action Limitations

**The Action Engine does NOT**:

- Automatically recommend "request refund" — resolution/redemption depends on Art. 119 conditions being met
- Automatically recommend "escalate" — escalation is a separate legal process
- Promise that any specific remedy will be granted — the system reports what MAY be applicable

**Missing action type**: The existing `REQUEST_REFUND` action type is not ideal for this module because the consumer's first remedy is repair/replacement, not refund. The system should use `CONTACT_MERCHANT` + `GENERATE_DOCUMENT` to request conformity, and `SUBMIT_COMPLAINT` for escalation. If `REQUEST_REFUND` is used, it must be clearly framed as "request resolution/price reduction" not "request refund" — the Action Engine description should reflect this.

## 11. Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Defect within 3 years, within 2-year presumption

- **Facts**: `purchase.delivery_date` = 14 months ago, `seller.rejection` = true, `nonconformity.description` = "screen flickers"
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED, Rule 2 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "Product is within the warranty period. The law presumes the defect existed at delivery."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the defect actually exists, whether the seller's rejection is justified

### Scenario 2: Defect after 3 years

- **Facts**: `purchase.delivery_date` = 37 months ago, `seller.rejection` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 1 NOT_APPLICABLE, Rule 2 NOT_APPLICABLE
- **System says**: "The legal responsibility period has expired."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the consumer has any remaining claim (prescription analysis under Art. 124 may still be relevant)

### Scenario 3: Defect within 2 years

- **Facts**: `purchase.delivery_date` = 18 months ago, `seller.rejection` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED, Rule 2 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "Within the warranty period AND within the 2-year presumption."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the seller has rebutted the presumption

### Scenario 4: Defect after 2 years but within 3 years

- **Facts**: `purchase.delivery_date` = 30 months ago, `seller.rejection` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED, Rule 2 NOT_APPLICABLE
- **System says**: "Within the warranty period, but the 2-year presumption has expired. The consumer must prove the defect existed at delivery."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the consumer can meet this burden of proof

### Scenario 5: Seller rejects citing "incorrect use"

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.claimed_misuse` = true, `seller.rejection_reason` = "usó el producto de forma indebida"
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED (if within period), Rule 5 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "The seller claims the defect was caused by misuse."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the use was actually incorrect. The system preserves the seller's claim as a factual assertion. Within the 2-year presumption, the burden is on the seller to prove misuse.

### Scenario 6: Seller rejects without explanation

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.rejection_reason` = absent
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED (if within period), Rule 3 may be SUPPORTED (if no remedy offered)
- **System says**: "The seller rejected the warranty claim. No reason was provided."
- **Cannot conclude**: That the rejection is automatically illegal — only that it lacks a stated reason

### Scenario 7: Seller offers repair

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.offered_repair` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 3 NOT_APPLICABLE (seller offered a remedy)
- **System says**: "The seller has offered repair."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether repair is the consumer's preferred remedy, whether repair will be done properly

### Scenario 8: Seller offers replacement

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.offered_replacement` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 3 NOT_APPLICABLE
- **System says**: "The seller has offered replacement."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the replacement is adequate (same model, refurbished, etc.)

### Scenario 9: Repair impossible

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.rejection_reason` = "reparación imposible", `seller.offered_repair` = false, `seller.offered_replacement` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 3 NOT_APPLICABLE (seller offered replacement as alternative)
- **System says**: "The seller claims repair is impossible and has offered replacement."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether impossibility is genuine

### Scenario 10: Repair disproportionate

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.rejection_reason` = "costes desproporcionados", `seller.offered_repair` = false, `seller.offered_replacement` = false
- **Rules**: Rule 3 SUPPORTED (no remedy offered)
- **System says**: "The seller claims repair is disproportionate but has not offered replacement."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether disproportionate costs is justified. Art. 118.3 requires the seller to consider the alternative remedy — if both are refused, Art. 119 may apply.

### Scenario 11: Repair fails

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `repair.completed` = true, `repair.failed` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 4 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "Repair was attempted but failed. Price reduction or resolution may be available under Art. 119."
- **Cannot conclude**: That resolution is the appropriate remedy (minor importance exception)

### Scenario 12: Same defect recurs after repair

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `repair.completed` = true, `repair.defect_recurred` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 4 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "The same defect recurred after repair. Price reduction or resolution may be available under Art. 119.d."
- **Cannot conclude**: That resolution is automatic. The consumer must have requested it (Art. 119.b).

### Scenario 13: Used product

- **Facts**: `product.is_used` = true, `purchase.delivery_date` = 14 months ago, `seller.rejection` = true, no agreed reduced period
- **Rules**: Rule 1 SUPPORTED, Rule 2 SUPPORTED (default 3-year/2-year periods apply when no agreement)
- **System says**: "The product is used, but no reduced warranty period was agreed. The standard 3-year/2-year periods apply."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether a reduced period was agreed but not documented

### Scenario 14: Commercial warranty exists

- **Facts**: `commercial_warranty.exists` = true, `commercial_warranty.guarantor` = "manufacturer", `seller.rejection` = true
- **Rules**: Commercial warranty tracked separately; does not affect legal warranty rules
- **System says**: "A commercial warranty from the manufacturer exists. This is separate from your legal warranty against the seller."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the commercial warranty covers the specific defect

### Scenario 15: No invoice but other proof

- **Facts**: `purchase.invoice_available` = false, alternative evidence exists (bank statement, email confirmation)
- **Rules**: Rules 1, 2 may evaluate if delivery date is established from alternative evidence
- **System says**: "No invoice available. Delivery date established from [alternative source]."
- **Cannot conclude**: The exact delivery date if alternative evidence is unreliable

### Scenario 16: Unknown delivery date

- **Facts**: `purchase.delivery_date` = absent
- **Rules**: Rules 1, 2 → INSUFFICIENT_DATA
- **System says**: "We cannot determine the warranty status without the delivery date."
- **Cannot conclude**: Anything about the warranty period

### Scenario 17: Contradictory facts

- **Facts**: User says delivery was "March 2024"; invoice says "January 2024"
- **Rules**: Rules 1, 2 → CONTRADICTED for `purchase.delivery_date`
- **System says**: "There is conflicting information about the delivery date. Please verify."
- **Cannot conclude**: Which date is correct

### Scenario 18: Seller vs. manufacturer

- **Facts**: Consumer bought from a store (seller), product made by a different company (manufacturer), claim filed against manufacturer
- **Rules**: Rules evaluate based on the claim target
- **System says**: "Your legal warranty claim is against the seller [name]. Art. 125 allows direct claims against the manufacturer in limited circumstances."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the manufacturer is the appropriate target (requires Art. 125 conditions)

### Scenario 19: Consumer requests resolution

- **Facts**: `consumer.resolution_declared` = true, `seller.rejection` = true, repair failed
- **Rules**: Rule 4 SUPPORTED (repair failed → Art. 119.d)
- **System says**: "Resolution may be available under Art. 119.d given the failed repair."
- **Cannot conclude**: That resolution will be granted — minor importance exception applies

### Scenario 20: Minor importance defect

- **Facts**: Defect is cosmetic (e.g., small scratch on non-visible part), `seller.rejection` = true
- **Rules**: Rules may evaluate as SUPPORTED for time-period rules, but the Result Engine must flag the Art. 119 limitation
- **System says**: "While the warranty period applies, resolution may not be available if the non-conformity is of minor importance (Art. 119, final clause)."
- **Cannot conclude**: Whether the defect is of minor importance — this is a qualitative judgment

### Scenario 21: Seller declares won't repair

- **Facts**: `seller.rejection` = true, `seller.declared_wont_repair` = true
- **Rules**: Rule 6 SUPPORTED
- **System says**: "The seller has declared they will not repair. Under Art. 119.f, price reduction or resolution may be available."
- **Cannot conclude**: That resolution is appropriate without evaluating minor importance

### Scenario 22: Repair done outside reasonable time

- **Facts**: `repair.completed` = true, `repair.within_reasonable_time` = false
- **Rules**: Rule 4 may evaluate if defect recurred or repair is considered failed due to delay
- **System says**: "Repair was not completed within a reasonable time."
- **Cannot conclude**: What constitutes a "reasonable time" (open legal question)

## 13. Open Legal Questions

### Non-Blocking (do not prevent V1 implementation)

1. **Nature-incompatibility exception** (Art. 121.1): When is the 2-year presumption "incompatible with the nature of the good or the nature of the non-conformity"? No definitive list exists. The system should flag this as a caveat when relevant, not encode it as a rule.

2. **"Disproportionate costs" threshold** (Art. 118.3): When are repair/replacement costs disproportionate? No quantitative threshold exists. The law considers value without defect and relevance of defect.

3. **"Reasonable time" for repair** (Art. 118.4.b): No specific duration defined. Case law and sector practice may provide guidance but cannot be codified as a rule.

4. **"Minor importance" exception** (Art. 119 final clause): When is a defect of minor importance? Entirely case-specific. The system must flag this as a limitation.

5. **Used goods: agreement on reduced period**: Art. 120.1 says "el empresario y el consumidor o usuario podrán pactar." Does this require explicit written agreement? The system defaults to 3 years when no agreement is documented.

6. **Directive 2024/1799 (Right to Repair)**: Must be transposed by 31 July 2026. May modify TRLGDCU. The system should monitor for legislative changes.

### No Blockers for V1

None of the open questions above prevent V1 implementation. The system is designed to:

- Flag open questions as caveats in results
- NOT encode unresolvable legal judgments as rules
- Present factual findings that enable human evaluation

### 15.3 Decisions Made

1. **Minimum required facts**: Reduced to 3 (`nonconformity.description`, `seller.response_received`, `seller.rejection`). This allows the intake to begin conversationally and only gather dates/details as needed.

2. **`product.type` as free text, not enum**: The intake should not force premature categorization. AI can suggest categories; the user confirms.

3. **6 rules, not 5**: Split the original "seller-refuses-all-remedies" into two rules (claims expired + declares won't repair) for cleaner condition evaluation.

4. **Derived facts for date computation**: The system computes deadline facts in `computeDerivedFacts` rather than inlining date arithmetic in rule conditions. This is consistent with the `no-delivery-refund` pattern.

5. **No new Action types**: The existing `ActionType` vocabulary is sufficient. The system uses `CONTACT_MERCHANT` + `GENERATE_DOCUMENT` for conformity requests, `SUBMIT_COMPLAINT` for escalation.

6. **V1 excludes digital conformity**: Goods with digital elements are included ONLY when the conformity issue is physical. Digital-specific obligations are excluded.

### 15.4 V1 Exclusions Summary

- Digital conformity obligations (Arts. 115 ter.2, 120.2, 121.2-5)
- Digital content/services remedies (Arts. 119 ter, 119 quáter)
- Damages computation
- Manufacturer subrogation management
- Cross-border purchases
- Time-limit suspension calculation
- Nature-incompatibility exception evaluation
- Minor-importance evaluation

### 15.5 Residual Risks

1. **Nature-incompatibility exception**: Some products (e.g., batteries, screens with inherent wear patterns) may trigger the Art. 121.1 exception. The system will present the 2-year presumption without caveat in most cases. This is acceptable for V1 because: (a) the exception is rare in common consumer goods, (b) the system correctly flags it as a limitation, and (c) the Result Engine's disclaimers provide appropriate hedging.

2. **Directive 2024/1799 transposition**: Spain must transpose by 31 July 2026. This may modify the TRLGDCU. The system should monitor for changes. This is a known future maintenance item, not a V1 blocker.

3. **"Reasonable time" for repair**: The system cannot evaluate this. It is flagged as an open question. For V1, this means the system cannot determine whether a repair delay violates Art. 118.4.b.

### 15.6 Questions That Do NOT Block Implementation

- Nature-incompatibility exception → flagged as caveat
- Disproportionate costs threshold → not evaluable by system
- Reasonable time for repair → not evaluable by system
- Minor importance exception → flagged as limitation
- Used-goods agreement form → default to 3 years when undocumented
- Directive 2024/1799 → future maintenance item
